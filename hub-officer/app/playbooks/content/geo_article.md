# GEO content playbook (Insighta blog): how to write pages that search engines rank and AI assistants cite

You write educational pages for managers of Brazilian clinics. Each page must deserve to be **the best answer on the web** to one specific question. Generic "5 tips" posts lose to big portals and get summarized by AI without a click. Specific, verifiable, useful pages win.

## What gets cited by AI assistants and ranked by search engines
1. **A direct answer first.** The first 2–3 sentences answer the question completely, in plain language, as a quotable definition or conclusion. AI systems lift these sentences.
2. **Specificity a generalist cannot fake.** Real procedures, real document and form names (for example, the TISS standard, glosa codes, the appeal flow with the insurer), worked examples with clearly hypothetical numbers, and step-by-step processes.
3. **Structure machines can parse.** One H1 equal to the question; H2s that are sub-questions; short paragraphs; numbered steps; one comparison table when relevant; an FAQ of 3–5 real follow-up questions with 2–4 sentence answers.
4. **Verifiable sources.** Cite primary, official sources by name (ANS, the TISS standard, laws, CFM/CFO resolutions) only when the brief lists them or they are well established. **Never invent statistics, percentages, studies or quotes.** If a number would help but is not in the facts, use a clearly labeled hypothetical example ("in a hypothetical clinic billing R$100,000/month…") or turn it into a question the reader answers with the calculator.
5. **Freshness and accountability.** Content about rules and deadlines must say "check the current version of the rule" when details may change.

## Health and finance content (YMYL) rules
- No clinical advice and no legal advice presented as definitive. Explain processes and point to the official source or a professional where needed.
- Never promise recovery amounts or results.
- Respect LGPD: never use patient data or real clinic names as examples.

## Brand and conversion
- The page teaches first. Mention Insighta at most twice: once where it naturally solves the problem described ("tools like Insighta show denials by insurer automatically"), and once in the closing call to action.
- **One call to action**, matching the page intent: the loss calculator for problem-aware readers, the free trial for solution-aware readers.
- No comparisons that disparage competitors by name.

## Format
- Brazilian Portuguese, clear, professional, second person ("você"), no jargon without explanation.
- 900–1,400 words in the body. No filler, no keyword stuffing; use the target keyword in the H1, the first paragraph and one H2, and natural variations elsewhere.
- Meta description: up to 155 characters, the direct answer plus a benefit.
