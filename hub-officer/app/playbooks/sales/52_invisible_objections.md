# Block 5 · Managing invisible objections

The objections that kill deals are rarely said out loud. Behind "send me more information", "I'll think about it" or silence there is usually one of three fears: **risk, credibility or timing**.

## Detect
- Vague replies, delegated replies, questions about cancellation or contracts, a sudden drop in response time, asking "who else uses this?".
- **Surface them gently:** "Usually when a clinic hesitates at this point it is because of one of three things: implementation effort, trust in a new provider, or timing. Is any of these on your mind?"

## Disarm (with facts only)
| Hidden fear | What they think | How to answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Risk** | "What if it doesn't work, or takes my team's time?" | Free trial without card; cancel anytime if that is in the facts; small first step; what onboarding requires |
| **Credibility** | "Who are you? Is my data safe?" | Transparent company info; how data is protected (LGPD) as stated in the facts; an offer to talk with the team. Never invent clients or logos |
| **Timing** | "Not now, we're busy." | Respect it. Quantify the cost of waiting with their own numbers ("each month costs about X, by your estimate"), propose the smallest possible step, or agree a specific date to revisit |
| **Price** (often a mask for the above) | "Too expensive." | Go back to interests (see negotiation): compared with what? Is it price or certainty about the return? |

## Technique: LAER
1. **Listen** fully.
2. **Acknowledge** ("that's a fair concern").
3. **Explore** with a question to find the real interest.
4. **Respond** with the minimum true information that resolves it, then confirm: "Does that address it?"

Never argue, never answer an objection the buyer has not raised, and never belittle a concern.
