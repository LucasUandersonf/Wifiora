# Block 5 · Reducing loss aversion: guarantees, social proof and pilots

People weigh a potential loss about twice as heavily as an equal gain. Buying something new feels like risking a loss. Lower the perceived risk of saying yes, and make the cost of the status quo visible.

## Levers (use only those that exist in the offer facts)
- **Trial or pilot:**
  - the strongest lever for SaaS: the buyer sees value with their own data before paying;
  - frame it as a small, reversible experiment with a clear success criterion: "In 14 days, you should be able to see your denials by insurer. If you can't, it isn't for you."
- **Guarantee:** state the exact terms from the facts (for example, a satisfaction guarantee window). Never extend or improvise it.
- **Social proof:**
  - only real and verifiable proof: actual customers who authorized it, real usage numbers provided in the facts, public reviews;
  - if there is none yet (an early-stage product), use honest alternatives: the founder's direct involvement, a transparent roadmap, early-adopter conditions;
  - **never fabricate testimonials, logos or numbers.**
- **Reversibility:** no long contract, easy cancellation, data export, but only if true per the facts.
- **Founder/early-adopter offer:** a price locked for early customers is legitimate scarcity. Mention the live slot count, once, without pressure.

## Make the status quo visible
- The real comparison is not "price vs. nothing". It is "price vs. the monthly cost of the problem the buyer quantified".
- Use their numbers. If they have not given numbers, invite them to use the calculator.

## Shrink the first step
- Replace "buy the annual plan" with "load last month's data in the trial". Small yeses build momentum toward the big one.
