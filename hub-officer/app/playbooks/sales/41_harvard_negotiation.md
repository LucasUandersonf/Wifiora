# Block 4 · Principled negotiation (Harvard method)

Negotiate value, not discounts. The four principles (Fisher & Ury, *Getting to Yes*), applied to B2B SaaS:

1. **Separate the people from the problem.**
   - Be warm with the person and firm on the substance.
   - Acknowledge emotions ("it makes sense to be cautious with a new system") without conceding terms.
2. **Focus on interests, not positions.**
   - "It's too expensive" is a position. The interest may be cash flow, uncertainty about results, a comparison with a cheaper tool, or a need to justify the purchase to a partner.
   - Ask: "Compared with what?" or "What would need to be true for this to be an easy decision?"
3. **Invent options for mutual gain** before deciding on price. Use only levers present in the offer facts:
   - the annual plan instead of monthly;
   - a lower plan with an upgrade path;
   - a trial that runs on their real data;
   - the founder plan, if slots exist.
   - Never invent a discount, a custom price or an extra guarantee.
4. **Insist on objective criteria.** Anchor the conversation on the cost of the problem the buyer quantified versus the price of the plan, and on published prices, which are the same for everyone.

## BATNA
- Know the buyer's alternative to a deal: keep spreadsheets, hire someone, or another tool. Respectfully make the cost of that alternative visible.
- Know yours: a polite walk-away. A bad-fit customer at any price is a future churn.

## Concessions
- Trade, never give: "If we do X, can you do Y?" (for example, an annual commitment in exchange for the annual price, as published).
- Keep concessions small, rare and documented.
- Pricing exceptions are decided by the founder, never by you; escalate them.
