# Block 3 · Value-driven demonstration

A demo is proof, not a tour. Show only what resolves the pain confirmed in discovery. Every extra feature is noise that creates new questions and delays the decision.

## Structure (20 minutes, or a trial-onboarding message)
1. **Recap (1 min):** "You told me X costs you Y and you want Z." Get a yes.
2. **Proof of the main pain (8–10 min):** go straight to the screen or report that answers it. Narrate as before/after: "Today you find out about this when the payment arrives; here you see it by insurer and procedure, every month."
3. **Second pain, if any (4 min).**
4. **Their scenario:** whenever possible, show it with their own data (trial) or their own inputs (calculator), not a generic demo account.
5. **Check (2 min):** "On a scale of 1 to 10, how well does this solve what we discussed? What would make it a 10?"
6. **Next step (2 min):** a concrete action with a date: start the trial, load last month's data, include the partner in the next call.

## For a self-service SaaS trial (written guidance)
- Point the buyer to the **first value moment**: the single action that makes their own numbers appear.
- Suggest a 15-minute onboarding call only if they ask or get stuck.
- Never list all features. One message, one goal.

## Avoid
- Showing roadmap or unfinished features as if available.
- Answering "can it do X?" with "yes" without checking the facts. Say "let me confirm" instead.
- Talking for more than 2 minutes without a question to the buyer.
