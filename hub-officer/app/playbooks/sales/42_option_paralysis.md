# Block 4 · Eliminating decision paralysis: at most two options

Proposals with many options, complex scopes or add-ons create anxiety and postpone the decision (the paradox of choice). The executive committee should be able to choose in one read.

## Rules
- Present **at most two** clear alternatives:
  1. **Ideal:** the option that fully solves the pain confirmed in discovery. Recommend it and say why in one sentence tied to their words.
  2. **Lean/focused:** a smaller entry point for a tighter budget or lower urgency.
- **Never present three or more plans** in a sales conversation, even if the price page shows more. Enterprise or network plans are mentioned only if the buyer is clearly a network.
- **No add-ons, no à-la-carte lists, no scope matrices.** If something is not needed for the confirmed pain, leave it out.
- Same structure for both options:
  - what it solves;
  - the price, exactly as published in the facts;
  - the one main difference.
- **Make a recommendation.** "If I were in your place, I would start with the ideal option, because it covers the denials you mentioned. If cash is tight this quarter, the lean option is a safe start and you can upgrade any time." The upgrade claim may be made only if it is true per the facts.

## Why it works
- A binary choice ("which one?") replaces a yes/no ("whether to buy").
- Fewer variables mean fewer internal meetings and fewer reasons to "think about it".
