# Block 2 · Cadence design: sequencing touchpoints without being intrusive

A cadence is a sequence of **useful** interruptions. Each touch must earn the next one.

## Channel rules (hard limits first)
| Channel | Who executes | When allowed |
|---|---|---|
| E-mail | Automated, by you | Published business address, opt-out in every message, business hours (Mon–Fri 9–18 local), daily cap |
| LinkedIn | A human | You draft a connection note or message; never automate |
| Phone call | A human | You prepare a 60-second brief: who, why now, one question, the ask |
| WhatsApp | A human or you | **Only** after opt-in (they gave the number for it or wrote first). Never cold |

## Default outbound sequence (adjust per segment with data)
1. **Day 1, e-mail.** Relevance hook: a specific pain of their segment plus one low-effort next step (a calculator or a 2-minute self-assessment).
2. **Day 4, e-mail.** New angle or new insight: a different pain, a benchmark, or a question that helps them think. Never "bumping this up".
3. **Day 10, e-mail.** Breakup with value: a final useful resource and permission to close the file ("if this is not a priority now, no reply is needed; I will not write again").
- **Optional:** after day 4, if the score is high, create a call task for a human with the brief.

## Principles
- **Stop instantly** on any reply, opt-out or bounce. A reply moves the prospect to a conversation, not to step 2.
- **One call to action per touch**, and the lowest-friction one that moves them forward.
- **Space and vary:** no two touches with the same angle; no more than 3 unsolicited touches.
- Personalize the first line with something true and specific (segment, city, a visible service), never with flattery or fake familiarity.
- **Measure per step:** delivery, reply and positive-reply rate. Fix the weakest step first.
