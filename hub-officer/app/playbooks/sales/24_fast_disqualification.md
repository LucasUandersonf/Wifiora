# Block 2 · Fast disqualification (strict qualification filter)

Top closers do not try to sell to everyone. They **disqualify fast**: if the lead has no urgent pain, no budget, or no technical/operational fit, the process ends within the first exchange. This protects your time, the pipeline's accuracy and, above all, the close rate. Proposals go only to opportunities with a real chance of closing.

## The three gates (all must be open to keep investing)
1. **Urgent pain:** the buyer recognizes a problem that costs money or control *now*. "It would be nice to have" is not pain.
2. **Capacity:** the business can afford the plan without stretching. Size signals: number of professionals or units, ticket, insurance billing volume.
3. **Fit:** our product solves *their* version of the problem with what exists today (no roadmap promises). Also check that the decision is taken here (not at a franchise HQ) and that they have data to put in.

## How to apply in a conversation (respectful, never rude)
- Ask the gate question early and directly: "Out of curiosity, how much does this problem cost you per month today, even as a rough guess?" or "Who besides you decides on a tool like this?"
- When a gate is clearly closed, **say so and step out**: "Honestly, given what you described, I don't think Insighta would pay for itself for you right now. I'd rather not take your time. If X changes, let me know." This honesty builds the brand and often brings referrals.
- When a gate is unclear, ask **one** more question. Do not build a proposal on hope.

## Signals to disqualify
- No pain articulated after the discovery question, or "just curious / collecting quotes" with no timeline.
- Too small for the cheapest plan to make sense, or no data to analyze.
- A requirement we do not meet today.
- The decision belongs to someone unreachable and the contact refuses to involve them.

## Output when you are asked to judge qualification
Return `qualified: false` with a short factual reason (for the CRM and the weekly lost-reasons review) whenever a gate is clearly closed. A clean "no" is a success.
