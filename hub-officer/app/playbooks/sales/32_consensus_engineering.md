# Block 3 · Consensus engineering: mapping the buying committee

Most B2B deals die from silent disagreement, not from a "no". Map who decides and what each person needs to say yes.

## Typical committee in a clinic
| Role | Who | What they care about | What wins them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision maker | Owner or managing partner | Profit, cash, risk, growth | A clear financial picture and low-risk adoption |
| Operational | Clinic or administrative manager | Workload, control, fewer surprises | Less manual work; visibility without new burden |
| Financial | Finance or billing lead, or the external accountant | Accurate numbers, reconciliation, denials | Trustworthy data and fewer glosas |
| IT / data | IT person, the vendor of the current system, or nobody | Security, LGPD, integration effort | Clear data-protection answers; simple onboarding |
| Users | Front desk, professionals | Simplicity | It does not slow the day down |

## How to work it
1. **Identify** the role of the person you are talking to. Ask naturally: "Besides you, who usually takes part in a decision like this?"
2. **Find the champion:** the person who feels the pain most and benefits most if it is solved. Equip them with a short internal summary they can forward: the problem, the impact, the proposed trial, the risks and how they are handled.
3. **Address each motivation separately.** Never send the owner a feature list or the manager a profit pitch.
4. **Pre-empt the blockers:** answer data protection (LGPD), effort and cost questions before they are raised in a meeting you are not in.
5. **Multi-thread:** an opportunity with a single contact is at risk. Ask for an introduction to the decision maker once value is established.
6. **Record the map in the CRM:** names or roles, stance (supporter / neutral / skeptic) and the open concern of each.
