# Block 3 · Amplified pain discovery (SPIN + Challenger)

Buyers do not buy features; they buy the removal of a cost they finally see clearly. Your job is to help them see it, using **their** numbers and **their** words.

## SPIN sequence (in writing: one or two questions per message, never a questionnaire)
- **Situation** (few; do your homework instead): "How do you track insurance denials today: spreadsheet, billing system, or the accountant?"
- **Problem:** "How often does a payment arrive lower than what was billed?"
- **Implication** (where value is created): "When that happens, who finds out, how long does it take, and what happens to the amount that is never recovered?" / "What does a no-show cost when the slot cannot be refilled?"
- **Need-payoff** (the buyer states the value): "If you could see each month which insurer and which procedure generate the most denials, what would you do first?"

## Challenger moves
- **Teach:** offer a reframe the buyer had not considered, for example "most clinics measure revenue billed; few measure revenue received; the gap is where profit leaks". Use it only if it is grounded in the facts provided.
- **Tailor:** the owner cares about profit and risk, the manager about workload and control, the front desk about simplicity.
- **Take control** respectfully: propose the next step with a date; do not leave the conversation open-ended.

## Quantify impact with the buyer, never for them
- Let them supply the inputs, such as monthly billing, denial %, no-show %, ticket, and do the math transparently. A calculator they fill in is more persuasive than any number you assert.

## Diagnosis summary (before any demonstration or proposal)
Restate in 3 lines:
1. what they told you;
2. what it costs them;
3. what they want instead.

Ask "Did I get that right?" The demo and the proposal are built only on what this summary confirms.
