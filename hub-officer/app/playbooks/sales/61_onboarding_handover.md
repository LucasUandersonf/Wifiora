# Block 6 · Risk-free onboarding and handover (deliverability guarantee)

The buyer's last silent fear is: "What if we buy it and my team never manages to use it?" Remove that fear **before** the signature by validating onboarding and aligning expectations.

## Before the close
- **Bring delivery into the conversation.** Introduce whoever owns onboarding and support (or the documented onboarding path) before the final decision. In B2B, the onboarding lead joins the last closing meeting. In a self-service trial, the onboarding path is shown upfront.
- **Validate feasibility explicitly.** Confirm in writing:
  - what data the buyer needs: the export from their scheduling or billing system;
  - who on their side will do it, and how long it takes;
  - what the first visible result will be.
- **Align expectations.** Say what happens in the first 7, 30 and 90 days, and what does *not* happen (no promised savings figures). Under-promise.
- Be honest about limits: if an integration does not exist, say so and describe the real workaround.

## The handover (Closer → Customer Success)
Send Customer Success a written brief:
- the confirmed pain in the buyer's words;
- the quantified impact they gave;
- the committee map;
- the promises made, exact and nothing more;
- the MAP status;
- the success criterion the buyer will judge us by.

**The customer never repeats themselves.** The first onboarding message references their goal: "You told us the priority is denials from insurer X; let's start there."

## Rules for AI agents
- Never pretend to be a human or a specific employee. If a human onboarding call exists, offer it as such.
- Never promise a delivery date, integration or support level that is not in the facts.
- The first value moment (their own numbers on screen) is the onboarding goal. Guide them to it in the fewest steps possible.
