# Block 1 · Process architecture: playbook, cadences and management rituals

## The sales playbook is the single source of truth
- **It contains:**
  - the ICP;
  - the personas and their pains;
  - the qualification criteria;
  - the stage definitions with entry and exit criteria;
  - the approved messages;
  - the objection map;
  - the offer facts.
- Every message you write must be traceable to the playbook. If reality contradicts the playbook, flag it, for example "clinics in segment X keep answering Y". Do not improvise policy.

## Stage definitions (an opportunity moves only when the exit criterion is met)
| Stage | Exit criterion |
|---|---|
| New | Company matches the ICP and has a published business contact |
| Contacted | First touch delivered |
| Engaged | Prospect replied (any intent except opt-out) |
| Qualified | Pain, owner and next step confirmed |
| Trial/Proposal | Prospect started a trial or received a proposal |
| Won / Lost | Paid, or explicit no / no response after the full cadence |

## Cadences
- **Outbound** (we start): 3 touches over about 10 business days by default. Each touch adds new value; never "just checking in". Stop on any reply.
- **Inbound** (they asked): respond within minutes during business hours. Speed to lead is the strongest single conversion lever. Inbound skips the cold sequence and goes straight to diagnosis.

## Management rituals (for the council and the dashboard)
- **Daily review (5 min):**
  - replies waiting for an answer;
  - trials started yesterday;
  - opportunities stuck more than 7 days in a stage;
  - deliverability (bounces, opt-outs).
- **Weekly review:**
  - funnel conversion by stage and by segment/city;
  - which message variant performs best;
  - the top 3 lost reasons;
  - one experiment for next week, with a single variable changed.
- **Monthly review:**
  - CAC vs. first-year value;
  - forecast accuracy;
  - ICP refinement.
- Each ritual ends with **decisions and owners**, not just observations.
